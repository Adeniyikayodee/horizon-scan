# Output spec

## Innovation map row

One row per approach, under its theme: theme, approach name, what it is, year,
overall fit, verification status, source link. Every row rests on a primary
source with a title, a link, and a date.

## Theme scorecard

One row per theme: name, standing (existing, adjacent, new), the four marks,
posture, whether it is a top area, and a marquee approach. One intro paragraph
on top.

## Memo shape

Two to three pages: the leading approaches grouped by theme with their postures,
the new and adjacent areas worth taking forward, and the open questions. Lead
with the two cleanest new areas.

## Voice and framing

Write as an experienced economic development expert whose focus is human
development and economic prosperity, so the reader feels the significance of each
finding, not only its facts. Match the register, the depth, and the house style of
the gold-standard exemplar provided: mature, informed, specific, and complete.

- Make the sense-making explicit. For every approach and every theme, say plainly
  why it matters for jobs, incomes, productivity, value addition, and human
  well-being in Africa, and how it moves evidence into policy and practice.
- Be specific, not generic. Name the institutions, the programs, the countries,
  and the figures. A sentence that could be said of any theme is not worth writing.
- Frame the institute as the one that defines and owns the value-capture agenda,
  the question of who keeps the value a new sector creates, not as a service
  downstream of the financiers, and use the word translator sparingly if at all.
- Read each finding through the DEPTH framework, naming the dimensions it advances.
- Connect related ideas into flowing, readable sentences joined by commas and the
  Oxford comma, rather than breaking each idea into a short sentence with a full
  stop. Every paragraph should earn its length by saying something specific.

## Naming

Name themes and approaches plainly and specifically, in the reader's own terms,
"Blue economy and coastal value addition", not a vague or clever label. A name
should tell a busy reader what the area is at a glance.

## House style (ACET)

- American (US) English, active voice, the Oxford (serial) comma.
- Prefer flowing sentences connected by commas; avoid short, clipped sentences.
- Spell out zero to nine, digits for 10 and up.
- Write out million, billion, trillion. "percent" in prose, % in tables.
- US dates (June 20, 2026). Double quotes in text, single in headings.
- No em dashes, use commas or rewrite.
- Write in the analyst's own voice. Describe findings, not how they were gathered.
- No trace of tools or AI, not in cells, prose, comments, or file properties.
- Author and last-modified are Kayode Adeniyi.
- Where a claim rests on secondary sources only, say so and mark it partial. Write "not found" where you cannot confirm, never invent.
