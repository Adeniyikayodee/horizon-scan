## Executive summary, the register to match

The African Center for Economic Transformation is building a Transformation Design
Hub to find new areas of focus, and this scan answers its central question, which
new areas the hub should move into, judged on their power to advance economic
transformation in Africa and to carry evidence into policy. The recommendation is
concentrated. Most of what the scan reviewed is work the hub already runs or should
simply keep watching, and a small set stands out as genuinely new ground worth
entering, led by two that are ready for the pipeline now, the blue economy, where
seaweed and aquaculture value addition open a coastal sector that African think
tanks have so far left aside, and the demand side of health manufacturing, where
the live opportunity is steady demand and active pharmaceutical ingredients rather
than another wave of plants. A single idea runs through the set, value capture,
Africa keeping more of the value it creates in a new sector, and it is the hub's to
own, because the capital and the technical knowledge already sit with the
financiers and the specialist providers, and the question none of them is asking is
who ends up capturing the value.

## A theme, the depth to match

Value addition in a coastal sector that African think tanks have so far left open
marks the opening in the blue economy, as seaweed processing and locally made
aquaculture feed turn marine resources into higher-value products on the continent,
carrying the same value-addition thesis the hub has long applied to minerals and
crops into a sector where the framing stands available. The technical and financing
weight already sits with others, the World Bank is backing Morocco's blue economy
and aquaculture as a route to growth and jobs, the African Development Bank runs a
fisheries-governance and value-chain program across the Southern African
Development Community region, and the United Nations Conference on Trade and
Development has set out seaweed's potential across its food, environmental, and
gender dimensions, while national strategies are already live, including Kenya's
National Blue Economy Strategy. Read through the DEPTH framework, the blue economy
advances diversification into a new productive sector beyond extractives, export
competitiveness in processed marine goods, and productivity in coastal value
chains, so its mandate fit is strong and its ground is genuinely new. The financiers
hold the projects and the money, which leaves the decisive question for the hub to
define and own, how an African economy retains more of the value its coast
produces, and a sound first step is a tightly scoped brief on the value-capture
policy that turns coastal activity into onshore processing, steady jobs, and public
revenue.
